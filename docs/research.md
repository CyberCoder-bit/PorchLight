This is the research behind PorchLight: how we chose the problem, the data, what already exists, and
the gap we're trying to fill. Part 2 of the submission (the narrative) summarizes it.

- **Secondary research (sections 2 and 3)** comes from public sources. Each number below was checked
  against the source page on **November 3, 2026**, and the exact wording is quoted so anyone can check
  it. If a number couldn't be confirmed on a page we could open, we left it out.
- **Primary research (sections 1, 4, and 5)** is what our team did ourselves. Anything marked
  **[TEAM: ...]** is still to be filled in by the team. Nothing in those sections has been made up:
  no quotes, results, or names.

## 3. What already exists, and the gap

These programs already do the most important part: real people reaching out to older adults. PorchLight
is **not** meant to replace them. It's designed as a tool a program like these could use. We have
**no partnership** with any of them. Descriptions come from each program's own public website,
checked November 3, 2026.

| Program | Who it serves | What it does (from its own site) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CICOA Aging & In-Home Solutions** | Older adults, people with disabilities, and caregivers in Marion, Boone, Hamilton, Hancock, Shelby, Johnson, Morgan, and Hendricks counties | Central Indiana's Area Agency on Aging. Its Aging & Disability Resource Center ((317) 803-6131 or (800) 432-2422) connects people with options counselors. Office hours are Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern. Services include home-delivered meals, transportation, caregiver support, and care management. | [S7], [S12], [S13] |
| **Shepherd's Center of Hamilton County** (Noblesville) | Adults 55+ in Hamilton County, free of charge | "Community Caring connects volunteers with home-bound, isolated, and/or financially fragile seniors 55+," including "friendly visitation." Its "Together Today" program works on "Preventing isolation and creating community for adults aged 55+." | [S14], [S15] |
| **PrimeLife Enrichment** (Carmel) | Calls itself the "Senior Center for Hamilton County" | A senior center on Third Avenue SW in Carmel with activities, fitness classes, aquatics, and transportation. Open Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. | [S16] |
| **AARP Community Connections: "Friendly Voice"** | Anyone who asks (national) | "Request a phone call from an AARP volunteer, or call us directly. Monday to Friday from 9AM to 5PM." | [S17] |
| **Indiana 211** (Indiana FSSA) | Any Hoosier | "a free information and referral service that connects Hoosiers with help." Dial 2-1-1 or 866-211-9966 (available 24/7). | [S18] |
| **Eldercare Locator** (U.S. Administration for Community Living) | Older adults and families nationwide | Connects people to local services for older adults: 1-800-677-1116. | [S19] |

**The gap PorchLight targets.** These programs connect volunteers and services with older adults.
None of their public pages describe either of these:

1. **A way for family to see whether contact is actually happening.** A grown child in another state
   usually finds out that calls stopped only when something goes wrong. PorchLight counts only
   completed calls and visits, and it flags "Isolation risk" and "Contact dropping" early.
2. **Help for the volunteer with what to say.** A teenager calling an 88-year-old can run out of
   things to say after "How are you?" PorchLight's Conversation Sparks give three starters based on
   the senior's interests and how long it's been since the last call.

Being fair about it: a website not mentioning a feature doesn't prove the program lacks it. Staff may
track calls internally or coach volunteers in training. [TEAM: ask about this in your interviews
(questions A4-A6 below). If a program already does one of these, say so in the narrative and explain
how PorchLight is different or would help them.]

**Also note:** the State of Aging report found that only 14% of older adults (55+) in Central Indiana have
used a senior center (D11). Many isolated seniors don't come to programs, so programs have to reach
out to them. That outreach (calls and visits) is the part PorchLight helps keep going.

## 4. Interview guide

**Before every interview:** say who you are and why you're asking (a school CS for Good project). Ask
whether you may take notes, and whether you may quote them by role (for example, "a volunteer
coordinator"). Don't record audio without clear permission. Never collect a senior's private details.
Write down answers in their words, and note the date and the person's role.

### A. Senior center or Area Agency on Aging staff member (10 questions, about 15 minutes)

1. How do you find out that an older adult is isolated or lonely?
2. Which programs here connect volunteers with seniors (calls, visits, rides)? About how many pairs
   are active?
3. When a volunteer match stops working, what usually happened? How long before you notice?
4. How do you track whether calls and visits actually happen? (Paper, spreadsheet, a database, or
   not at all?)
5. Do families ever ask how often their parent is hearing from someone? What do you tell them?
6. What training or help do volunteers get about what to talk about?
7. How do you decide which volunteer to pair with which senior?
8. What would make you NOT want to use a tool like this? (Privacy, time, cost, training?)
9. Look at this screenshot of the dashboard (or the "Needs attention" list). What's confusing? What's
   missing?
10. Who else should we talk to?

### B. Volunteer who calls or visits older adults (8 questions, about 10 minutes)

1. How did you start volunteering, and how often do you call or visit?
2. What was hard about the first call?
3. Has a call ever felt awkward or run out of things to say? What did you do?
4. Have you ever stopped calling someone or called less often? What got in the way?
5. How do you remember when your next call is and what you talked about last time?
6. Look at these three conversation starters. Would you use them? What would make them better?
7. Would a one-click "Log this call" button be easy to use right after a call?
8. What would you want a coordinator to know about you, so they can match you well?

### C. Family caregiver (a grown child or relative of an older adult) (9 questions, about 10 minutes)

1. How often do you talk to your parent or relative, and how?
2. How do you know whether they've talked to anyone else this week?
3. Has there been a time you worried they'd gone days without talking to anyone? How did you find out?
4. If a volunteer called them every week, what would you want to know about those calls?
5. What would be too much information? (We never share call notes with families.)
6. Look at this dashboard card. What does "Isolation risk" mean to you? Is the wording clear?
7. How many days without contact would worry you for your parent?
8. Would you want a weekly summary you could forward to siblings?
9. Is there anything about privacy that would make you or your parent uncomfortable?

who speaks a language they share. This section shows where the language list came from. All
numbers were checked on **November 4, 2026**. "LEP" below means people who told the Census they
speak English less than "very well". They are the people most likely to need the app in another
language.

### What the Census can't show: Burmese and Chin in Indianapolis

The ACS doesn't list Burmese or any Chin language by name. They are inside "Other languages of
Asia" (23,308 speakers in Indiana). Local sources fill the gap:

- The *Encyclopedia of Indianapolis* says "Indiana is home to the greatest number of Burmese
  refugees in the United States", that "by 2020, Exodus had placed almost 20,000 Burmese in
  Indianapolis", that "the Chin make up 75 percent" of them, and that "most are in Perry Township
  and Southport" [L5].
- *Indianapolis Monthly* (December 12, 2020) says "almost 20,000 Chin live on the south side" [L6].
- *Chinland Guardian* (January 30, 2013) reported that the Burmese American Community Institute
  (BACI), "founded in 2011", opened a center at the Perry Township Government Center, and that
  Indianapolis was then "home to approximately 9,000 Chin refugees" [L7].

These counts come from refugee placements and reporting, not from the Census, and we couldn't
confirm a current total. We also couldn't confirm how many of these Hoosiers speak Hakha Chin
in particular, as opposed to other Chin languages such as Falam, Tedim, or Zomi.

### Pennsylvania Dutch (the Amish)

The Census puts Pennsylvania Dutch in one group with Yiddish and other West Germanic languages
(37,403 speakers in Indiana). The Young Center for Anabaptist and Pietist Studies estimates
29,180 Amish in the Elkhart/LaGrange Counties settlement and 10,855 in the Adams County
settlement in 2024 [L8]. We couldn't confirm how many of them speak Pennsylvania Dutch at home.

### The 8 interface languages we chose

English and Spanish had to be on the list, and so did one right-to-left language. Our first draft
list was English, Spanish, Arabic, Burmese, Simplified Chinese, Vietnamese, French, and German. The
data agreed with seven of them and changed one.

| Code | Language | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `en` | English | Most Hoosiers speak it. It's the default. |
| `es` | Español (Spanish) | The largest by far: 161,812 LEP speakers. |
| `zh-Hans` | 中文（简体）(Simplified Chinese) | Largest LEP group after Spanish: 13,715. |
| `my` | မြန်မာ (Burmese) | Census group 12,558 LEP, and about 20,000 Burmese refugees in Indianapolis [L5]. Burmese is Myanmar's official language. We couldn't confirm how many Chin Hoosiers read it. |
| `ht` | Kreyòl ayisyen (Haitian Creole) | 11,004 LEP, ahead of French (3,674). This replaces French from the draft. |
| `de` | Deutsch (German) | 40,333 speakers and 8,057 LEP, plus the West Germanic group above. |
| `ar` | العربية (Arabic) | Our right-to-left language. 4,402 LEP (±1,795). |
| `vi` | Tiếng Việt (Vietnamese) | 4,228 LEP (±1,421). |

Vietnamese, Korean (4,458), Punjabi (4,609), and French (3,674) are close together, and their
margins of error overlap, so the data can't tell them apart. We kept Vietnamese from the draft
list. Korean, Punjabi, French, Hindi, Telugu, Tagalog, and the others are on the spoken-language
list, so they still count for matching.

Hakha Chin is on the spoken-language list only. Google Translate added
Hakha Chin in June 2024 [L9]. Machine translation for it is new, and we have no Chin speaker to
check a translated interface. We won't ship a Hakha Chin interface we can't check. A senior can
still list Hakha Chin as a language they speak, so they can be paired with a companion who speaks
it. Pennsylvania Dutch is spoken-only for the same reason.

Every interface language other than English is machine-translated. Each catalog file says so at
the top and needs a native speaker to check it before real use.
